\begin{abstract}
Real-time risk analysis of credit for enterprises using financial systems requires the continuous and efficient processing of multiple data streams, including transactional activity, external financial indicators, and litigation-related signals. Current credit monitoring approaches typically rely on periodic batch analysis or manual review by credit control teams, thereby preventing early intervention when there is a decline in credit. This paper presents ACIS-X, an Autonomous Credit Intelligence System built as a distributed multi-agent framework upon Apache Kafka event streaming. ACIS-X decomposes the credit intelligence lifecycle into twelve specialized, loosely coupled agents spanning behavioral metrics computation, external signal enrichment, multi-tier risk fusion, policy enforcement, collections routing, and autonomous self-healing. A novel three-tier signal fusion model combines transactional behavioral metrics with external financial indicators and litigation intelligence into a unified risk score. Furthermore, the system guarantees regulatory compliance via SHAP-based model explainability persistence. The self-healing orchestration layer monitors agent health via heartbeat telemetry and autonomously restarts failed components with zero event loss, leveraging Kafka offset-based recovery. Experimental evaluation using 162 automated tests covering unit, integration, self-healing proof, and performance scenarios demonstrates: pipeline P95 latency under 500\,ms across 500 events, mean time to recovery of under 30\,s across 50 fault-injection cycles, exact-once deduplication verified over 10,000 repeated events, and near-linear horizontal throughput scaling exceeding 2.5$\times$ baseline at four replicas. Risk score monotonicity, null-safety under partial signal availability, and thread-safe concurrent rebuilds are formally validated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ============================================================

Management of enterprise credit risks calls for constant evaluation of payment status, financial position, and potential litigations. The traditional model works with daily or weekly cycles that introduce delays which do not enable immediate action in case of increased risk throughout the day. With the emergence of event streaming technologies like Apache Kafka~\cite{kreps2011kafka} and new developments in multi-agent software development~\cite{zhang2024event}, there is a possibility of changing the architecture of credit intelligence fundamentally.

This work introduces ACIS-X (Autonomous Credit Intelligence System, Extended), which is a fully developed system written in Python~3.10 and uses Apache Kafka~3.6 as its event foundation. ACIS-X tackles the following three main limitations of prior work:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Latency}: The batch systems do not capture the evolution of risk signals within a day essential for preemptive collections.
    \item \textbf{Siloed Signals}: Financial, behavioral, and litigation risk signals are independently evaluated, and therefore risk fusion is impossible.
    \item \textbf{Fragility}: The single-process pipeline does not have autonomous fail-over capabilities, leading to outages upon agent crashes.
\end{enumerate}

The primary contributions of this work include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A distributed 12-agents architecture partitioned in five functional layers, where all layers communicate solely through Kafka topics.
    \item A three-layer signal fusion system that takes on-time payments rate, age of receivables, outside financial metrics (debt/eq, ROE, PE) and litigation impact into account to form a risk score using validated weights.
    \item An autonomous self-healing orchestration layer with heartbeat-based failure discovery and Kafka offset based zero loss agent recovery.
    \item Experimental proof by running 162 automatic tests: P95 latency of pipeline $<500$\,ms, mean time to repair $<30$\,s in a fault injection cycle, perfect-once deduplication for 10k events and $\geq2.5\times$ horizontal scalability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{Traditional credit scoring} methods such as Altman's Z-score~\cite{altman1968financial} and logistic regression models~\cite{wang2024explainable} rely on annual financial statements, rendering them structurally incapable of intraday risk tracking. Ensemble methods~\cite{li2024ensemble} and LSTM-based approaches~\cite{chen2025streaming} improve accuracy but remain batch-oriented with hourly or daily refresh cycles.

\textbf{Streaming financial systems} demonstrate Kafka's suitability for high-throughput financial processing~\cite{kreps2011kafka}, with fraud detection systems achieving sub-second latency~\cite{liu2025realtime}. However, comprehensive credit health monitoring combining behavioral, financial, and litigation signals in a unified streaming pipeline has not been demonstrated.

\textbf{Multi-agent systems} have been applied to financial market simulation~\cite{zhao2024multiagent} and compliance monitoring, with Kumar et al.~\cite{kumar2024engineering} providing foundational agent-oriented engineering methodology for FinTech. No prior work demonstrates MAS applied to streaming credit intelligence with integrated self-healing at production scale.

\textbf{Self-healing systems} grounded in the MAPE-K loop~\cite{singh2025selfhealing} and chaos engineering principles~\cite{patel2024chaos} provide coarse-grained recovery via container restart policies. Fine-grained, domain-aware agent restart protocols preserving event processing state -- as implemented in ACIS-X -- have not been demonstrated in financial AI contexts.

The critical gap addressed by ACIS-X lies at the intersection of real-time event streaming, multi-agent signal fusion, and autonomous self-healing, none of which prior works combine in a unified, production-validated system.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}
%% ============================================================

Let $\mathcal{C} = \{c_1,\ldots,c_N\}$ be enterprise customers in a credit relationship. For each customer $c_i$, the system must maintain a continuously updated risk score:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}(c_i,t) = \mathcal{F}\bigl(\mathcal{B}(c_i,t),\;\mathcal{E}(c_i,t),\;\mathcal{L}(c_i,t)\bigr)
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{B}$ captures behavioral signals from transactional history, $\mathcal{E}$ captures external financial indicators, and $\mathcal{L}$ captures litigation exposure. The fusion function $\mathcal{F}: \mathbb{R}^p\times\mathbb{R}^q\times\mathbb{R}^r \to [0,1]$ maps heterogeneous signals to a scalar risk score. Key constraints are: (1)~end-to-end latency $\tau_{\text{e2e}}\leq\delta_{\max}=1{,}000$\,ms; (2)~valid risk estimates under partial signal availability; (3)~zero event loss under agent failure; and (4)~idempotent processing of duplicate events.

\subsection{Kafka Partitioning and Consumer Group}

Message keys are based on the \texttt{customer\_id} hash such that messages are delivered to individual customers in order. Different types of agents run independently in their own dedicated consumer groups (\texttt{customer-state-group}, \texttt{external-data-group}, \texttt{risk-scoring-group}, \texttt{collections-group}). There are three partitions per topic, and thus three replicas of each agent can reside in the same consumer group.

\subsection{Persistence Layer}

The SQLite schema comprises ten tables: \texttt{customers}, \texttt{invoices}, \texttt{payments}, \texttt{customer\allowbreak \_risk\allowbreak \_profile}, \texttt{customer\_metrics}, \texttt{external\allowbreak \_financials} (90-day TTL cache), \texttt{external\allowbreak \_litigation} (90-day TTL cache), \texttt{risk\allowbreak \_explanations} (SHAP audit log), \texttt{collections\_log}, and \texttt{event\_log}. DBAgent uses \texttt{INSERT OR\allowbreak\ REPLACE} for idempotent upserts. MemoryAgent maintains an in-memory customer state derived from database queries, providing fast access to aggregated metrics with thread-safe state management via \texttt{threading.Lock}.

\section{Methodology and Mathematical Model}
\label{sec:methodology}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Three-Tier Signal Fusion}

\textbf{Tier 1 -- Behavioral Signals} ($\mathcal{B}$): Computed by CustomerStateAgent via a single bulk invoice query per event, with per-customer thread locking to prevent concurrent rebuild race conditions. The on-time ratio and average delay are:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\text{OTRatio}(c_i) &= \frac{|\{I_k : \text{paid}(I_k)\leq\text{due}(I_k)\}|}{|\mathcal{I}(c_i)|}, \\
\text{AvgDelay}(c_i) &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{k}\max\bigl((\text{paid}_k - \text{due}_k),\,0\bigr)
\end{split}
\label{eq:behavioral}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Tier 2 -- External Signals} ($\mathcal{E}$, $\mathcal{L}$): ExternalDataAgent queries financial APIs for debt/equity ratio, ROE, PE ratio, and sales growth, cached for 90 days. ExternalScrapingAgent queries legal news aggregation APIs with a 365-day lookback window, classifying litigation status as active, stayed, or resolved. Both agents publish to \texttt{acis.metrics} with \texttt{external.\allowbreak data.\allowbreak enriched} and \texttt{external.\allowbreak litigation.\allowbreak updated} event types respectively.

\textbf{Tier 3 -- Signal Fusion} (AggregatorAgent): Financial and litigation signals are fused as:

\begin{equation}
\text{CombinedRisk}(c_i) = 0.6\cdot\mathcal{E}(c_i) + 0.4\cdot\mathcal{L}(c_i), \quad \text{CombinedRisk}\in[0,1]
\label{eq:aggregation}
\end{equation}

AggregatorAgent applies event freshness guards (discarding stale events older than a cached entry) and change-detection deduplication: a new \texttt{risk.\allowbreak profile.\allowbreak updated} event is published only when either financial or litigation risk values change from the last published values.

\subsection{Payment Risk Prediction}

PaymentPredictionAgent computes a per-invoice risk score using an explicit two-stage model consisting of a machine learning base and policy overrides. The first stage ($r_{ml}$) is a Random Forest ensemble:

\begin{equation}
r_{ml} = P(\text{Default}|X) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} h_m(X)
\label{eq:prediction}
\end{equation}

where $M=50$ is the number of decision trees, and the feature vector $X$ incorporates both continuous behavioral metrics and context variables (e.g., $s_{\text{delay}}$, credit utilization, on-time ratio, litigation risk, and financial health). 

To achieve systematic reliability, ACIS-X follows this dual-pipeline approach. The machine learning part can sense any changes in the continuous behavioral patterns, whereas the policy part ($r_{policy}$) penalizes the risk associated with any critical events (for example, lawsuits or  sudden credit rating drops):

\begin{equation}
r_{final} = \max(0, \min(1, r_{ml} + r_{policy}))
\label{eq:twostage}
\end{equation}

To meet regulatory standards and keep the algorithm completely transparent, ACIS-X calculates exact SHAP values using the TreeExplainer method \cite{lundberg2020local}. The model works by breaking down the final risk score into a baseline expected value, $E[f(X)]$, and individual feature attributions, $\phi_i$:

\begin{equation}
f(X) = E[f(X)] + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \phi_i
\label{eq:shap}
\end{equation}

For example, a high-risk prediction ($0.80$) might be broken down transparently as: \textit{Baseline (0.45) + Payment Delay (+0.20) + High Utilization (+0.10) + Active Litigation (+0.05)}. The system saves these SHAP values directly to the \texttt{risk\allowbreak \_explanations} table, allowing auditors to trace exactly why it flagged a specific invoice for collections.

\subsection{Risk Refinement and Final Scoring}

The \texttt{RiskScoringAgent} refines the base prediction by analyzing five normalized behavioral factors alongside an external context boost:

\begin{equation}
r_{\text{adj}} = r_{\text{pred}} + 0.85\cdot\Delta_{\text{behavioral}} + 0.30\cdot\Delta_{\text{temporal}}
\label{eq:refinement}
\end{equation}

where $\Delta_{\text{behavioral}}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta_{\text{behavioral}} =\ &0.40\cdot a_{\text{overdue}} + 0.20\cdot a_{\text{payment}} \\
&+ 0.15\cdot a_{\text{delay}} + 0.15\cdot a_{\text{outstanding}} + 0.10\cdot a_{\text{exposure}}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here, each individual factor is normalized to $[0,1]$.To calculate the external context boost, the system blends aggregated, financial and litigation risks using structured weighting schemes (weights 0.40, 0.30, 0.20, 0.10), which applies up to a 20\% multiplicative boost to $\Delta_{\text{behavioral}}$. Meanwhile, temporal trend signals—specifically velocity, stability, and volatility—contribute $\Delta_{\text{temporal}}$,which the system detects via a \texttt{get\_risk\_velocity} query. The final score is clamped to $[0,1]$ and classified: high $>0.75$, medium $>0.40$, low otherwise.

\subsection{Self-Healing Protocol}

The SelfHealingAgent implements a MAPE-K control loop. Each agent publishes a heartbeat event every $T_H=5$\,s. Agent $a_j$ is declared failed when:

\begin{equation}
t_{\text{now}} - t_{\text{heartbeat}}(a_j) > \theta_{\text{timeout}} = 60\,\text{s}
\label{eq:heartbeat}
\end{equation}

Upon detection, the SelfHealingAgent emits an \texttt{agent.restart.requested} event, reinitializing the failed agent's Kafka consumer from its last committed offset. This guarantees zero message loss across all agent failure scenarios.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{Technology stack}: Python~3.10, \texttt{confluent-kafka}~2.3, SQLite~3 (stdlib), \texttt{pytest}~7.4, Docker Compose (Kafka~3.6 + ZooKeeper~3.8). The full system comprises approximately 12 agent modules totalling over 350\,KB of production Python code.

\textbf{Lazy producer initialization}: Kafka producers are created on the first \texttt{publish()} call per agent rather than at startup. This reduces startup Kafka connections from 20 to 2 (90\% reduction) and startup time from $>60$\,s to $<30$\,s.

\textbf{In-memory state management}: MemoryAgent maintains an event-driven, in-memory view of customer state derived from database queries, with thread-safe access via \texttt{threading.Lock} and O(1) idempotency checks using a hybrid Set+Deque guard (bounded to 10,000 entries).

\textbf{Idempotent deduplication}: DBAgent uses \texttt{INSERT OR\allowbreak\ REPLACE} with primary-key constraints. AggregatorAgent tracks last-published risk values per customer in an OrderedDict (bounded to 10,000 entries) and skips publication when values are unchanged. CollectionsAgent maintains a set-based guard preventing duplicate collection actions for the same (customer, invoice, action) triple.

\textbf{Cache management}: Financial and litigation data are cached in SQLite with 90-day TTL. AggregatorAgent enforces a 24-hour in-memory cache TTL with periodic cleanup every 60 seconds, bounded to 50,000 customers. RiskScoringAgent context cache enforces 24-hour TTL with 5-minute cleanup intervals, bounded to 10,000 customers.

\textbf{Thread safety}: CustomerStateAgent uses per-customer \texttt{threading.Lock()} instances for rebuild operations, preventing concurrent rebuild race conditions that caused cache drift in earlier versions.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ============================================================

\textbf{Fusion of signals contribution}: Ablation studies prove that external signals are important for performance. The freshness feature in \texttt{AggregatorAgent} prevents old cache values from overwriting fresh scores through null safety testing.

\textbf{Trade-offs of the architecture}: Choreography eliminates SPOFs, but it makes tracing complex and requires OpenTelemetry. SQLite works fine for local environment; production needs PostgreSQL using \texttt{DBAgent}.

\textbf{Self-healing of correctness}: Test proves that \texttt{SelfHealingAgent} avoids cascading restarts when agents fail simultaneously, and health routing works correctly across any agent state.

%% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ============================================================

ACIS-X achieves credit intelligence in real time through multi-agent decomposition on Kafka. The twelve-agent system, signal fusion, SHAP interpretability, and MAPE-K self-healing solve the problem of latencies and fragilities of batch systems. Our testing shows sub-500\,ms P95 latency, sub-30\,s MTTR, exactly-once deduplication, and near-linear scalability.

Our future work will include LSTM sequence modeling, federated learning, and graph-based supply chain risk modeling.
